*Key Concepts:*
___

We have that for compounding interest, the total interest gained over a period longer than its subperiod will be different than the subperiod interest.

Thus, if we want to change the interest period, we will also need to change the interest rate to maintain equivalence. This interest rate is called $i_{e}$, or interest effective. 

$F = P(1 + i_s)^m$
$F = P(1 + i_{s})^{\frac{m}{m'}\cdot m'}$
$F = P(1 + i_{e})^{m'}, \quad i_{e}=(1+i_{s})^{\frac{m}{m'}} - 1$

For the special case where m' = 1, then

$i_{e} = (1+i_s)^m$

Or in other words, $i_e$ is the total effective interest over m periods.

*Key Concepts:*
___

```ad-definition

Drawing general conclusions from specific observations. 

```

Inductive reasoning is useful to find patterns, but oftentimes is not rigorous. It is useful in physical sciences, problem solving, and as an intermediate step to a proof, but is not often used to write proofs. An exception is the formal technique of proof, [[Proof by Induction]].

For example, patterns can continue for a long time, but still eventually break. Inductive reasoning is unable to *prove* these patterns, only predict them.

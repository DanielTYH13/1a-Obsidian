*Key Concepts:*
___
**Definition:**

- The inverse of a function f(x) maps values of f(x) back to its input, x. 
- Along the [[Domain of Function Composition|appropriate domains]]: $f^{-1}(f(x)) = x = f(f^{-1}(x))$

- Graphically, the inverse of a function is its reflection along y = x, or f(x) = x:
	- This is supported by the definition of the inverse of a function.

---
**Function Invertibility:**

- A function is invertible $\iff$ the function passes the [[One to One Functions|HLT]].
	- Since domain and range swap, it must be 1:1 to prevent ambiguity in outputs.

---
**Finding the Inverse of a Function**:

- Swap x for y. Isolate for y.

- Reasoning:
	- Notice that the process of finding $f^{-1}(n)$, where $n \in \mathbb{R}$, involves setting f(x) = n and solving for x.
	- The generalization of this is to swap x and y, and solving for y. This is equivalent, but explicitly defines the new output as the inverse of any input x.

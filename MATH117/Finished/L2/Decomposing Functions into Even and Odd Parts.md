**Theorem:**

- Any function f(x) where $D_{f(x)}$ is symmetric across $x = 0$ can be represented as a **sum of even and odd parts**:

---
**Definition:**

- Assuming that the domain of f(x) is symmetric across $x = 0$, then:
	- $f(x) = \frac{1}{2}[f(x) + f(-x)] + \frac{1}{2}[f(x)-f(-x)]$

---
**Reasoning:**

- Using the definition that 
	- $f(x) = \frac{1}{2}[f(x) + f(-x)] + \frac{1}{2}[f(x)-f(-x)]$,
- Allow g(x) = f(x) + f(-x), and h(x) = f(x) - f(-x).
- Then, by the commutitive and distributive properties of addition:
	- g(x) is garunteed to be even.
	- h(x) is garunteed to be odd. 

---
**Derivation:**

1. $f(x) = f(x) + \frac{1}{2}f(-x) - \frac{1}{2}f(-x)$
2. $f(x) = \frac{1}{2}f(x) + \frac{1}{2}f(-x) + \frac{1}{2}f(x) - \frac{1}{2}f(-x)$
3. $f(x) = \frac{1}{2}[f(x) + f(-x)] + \frac{1}{2}[f(x) - f(-x)]$

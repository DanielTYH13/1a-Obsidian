*Key Concepts:*
___

Electric flux is defined as the surface integral of a surface with the electric field flowing through it. 

---
For the simplest case, consider an infinite plane charge source, emitting constant E across a plane. Then, $\Phi = EA$, as all of the electric fields are flowing tangentially across the plane.

To generalize for non planar surfaces, we can take the closed integral of the surface with respect to an infinitesimal plane, where we sum all $E_{T}dA$ together toget the surface flux of the non planar surface. 

Since we want only the normal component, we can extract that with $E \cdot \vec{n} = |\vec{n}||E|cos \theta = |E|cos \theta$

Thus, the total flux becomes $\displaystyle \int_{S} \vec{E} \cdot \vec{n} dA$. 

By associativity, we can let $\overline{dA} = dA \vec{n}$, which gives us $\Phi = \displaystyle \int_{S}\vec{E} \cdot \overline{dA}$

*Key Concepts:*
___

Given the most basic circuit, a battery connected with ideal conductors to a capacitor, you have the following:

The voltage at the terminals of the capacitors are equivalent to those at the battery (As the conductors have no E field, thus they are equipotential). 

The E field at the capacitor must be strong enough (Inversely proportional to its distance) to match the voltage of that at the battery. 

In other words:

$V_{capacitor} = V_{battery}$
$d_{c}E_{capacitor} = d_{b}E_{battery}$ where d is the distance between terminals. 

The RHS term is given by the constant E field defined by the axis between the battery nodes. The LHS term is given by the constant E field of a capacitor due to low distance from each plate.

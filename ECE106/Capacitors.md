*Key Concepts:*
___

Capacitors help stored energy in the form of charges over an electric field. C

The generic geometry of a capacitor are two conductive surfaces seperated by some distance, and in some cases, some medium. 

When a voltage is applied to either terminal of the capacitor with conductors, the conductive surfaces also then have a voltage equal to that supplied. 

Since E field is conservative, all charges passing from one surface to the other must experience an E field that satisfies $\Delta V = -\displaystyle \int^b_{a}\vec{E} \cdot \overline{dl}$ regardless of path, including the one passing through the wire and the capacitor. This necessitates charge difference on each end to create the field.

Since, depending on the capacitor's characteristics, e.g. the required E field goes down or the E field per coulomb goes down, the number of charge required to create any given voltage is different. (Distance up, Less E field required, less charge. Permittivity goes up, less E field per coulomb, more charge)

This is the capacitance, charge / v.

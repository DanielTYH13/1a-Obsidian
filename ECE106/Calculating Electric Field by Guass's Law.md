*Key Concepts:*
___

Gauss's Law give us:

$\displaystyle \int_{S} \vec{E}\cdot \overline{dA} = \frac{Q_{enclosed}}{\epsilon}$

However, we have that if $\vec{E}$ is uniform in direction relative to the normal everywhere (ie the normal component of the E field is constant everywhere), 

$\displaystyle \int_{S} \vec{E}\cdot \overline{dA} = \frac{Q_{enclosed}}{\epsilon}$
$\displaystyle \int_{S} \vec{E}\cdot \vec{n} dA = \frac{Q_{enclosed}}{\epsilon}$
$\vec{E} \cdot \vec{n} \displaystyle \int_{S} dA = \frac{Q_{enclosed}}{\epsilon}$
$\vec{E}\cdot \vec{n} = \frac{Q_{enclosed}}{\epsilon A}$

This means that for any point along the gaussian surface, the normal component of the electric field is equal to that.

Note that this tells use nothing about the electric field itself, unless it was already colinear with the normal in the first place.

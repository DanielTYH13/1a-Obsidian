*Key Concepts:*
___

The equation for the voltage due to a single point charge is given as:

$V = \frac{Q}{4\pi r \epsilon}$

---

Similar to E field, you can add the voltage due to a point charge together with others, or in an integral.

Unlike the E field, relative position of the point charges does not matter. This is because voltage is taken wrt infinity.

A good way of thinking of this is that at infinity, all points, no matter their positioning or distance form each other, look like a single point charge with Q = Q_1 + Q_2 + ... + Q_n.

Moreover, infinity is the same at all points regardless of the direction.

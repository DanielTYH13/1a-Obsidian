*Key Concepts:*
___

For a conductor with a cavity and an E field source outside of the cavity, the charge distribution on the surface of the cavity is neutral, and all other [[Conductors|conductor]] properties apply. 

The reasoning is as followings:

Assume there is a net E field within the cavity that is not 0. 

Then, by the definition of the conservative electric field, we have that for a point A on the cavity's surface, 

$\displaystyle -\oint^b_{a} \vec{E} \cdot \overline{dl} = 0$, which must be true path independant. 

However, consider the following path: 

![[Pasted image 20260621165834.png|200]]

In this case, because net field != 0, then the voltage changes from A to B, and since E field within the conductor = 0, the field cannot be conservative. Thus there is a contradiction, and therefore the net field within the cavity of a conductor in equilibrium MUST be 0.

Note that this is Net field across the entirety of the cavity, not at any point. There can exist a field within the cavity, it needs only be that the total field experienced across any path through the cavity must be 0.

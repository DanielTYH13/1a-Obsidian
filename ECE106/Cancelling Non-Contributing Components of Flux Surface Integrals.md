*Key Concepts:*
___
It is a property of integrals that they are linear. Thus $\displaystyle \int (S_{1}+S_{2}) = \int S_{1} + \int S_{2}$. This applies to gaussian surfaces as well. Thus, in cases where the dot product of $E \cdot \vec{n}$ is 0, you can simply eliminate them - especially if they are pesky geometries.

We need only find the flux for the end caps, which becomes trivially easy. They resolve simply to $|\vec{E}| dA$ for both sides, giving a total of $2|\vec{E}|dA = \frac{Q}{\epsilon_0}$

Thus, $|\vec{E}| = \frac{Q}{2\epsilon_{0}dA}$

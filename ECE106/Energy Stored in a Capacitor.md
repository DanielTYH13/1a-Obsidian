	*Key Concepts:*
___

When neutral, there are an equal amount of protons and electrons at either ends of the capacitor. 

In order to charge the capacitor, you must move an electron from the positive terminal to the negative. However, to do this, you must enact some work to counteract the E field, which is the voltage:

$du = dq \Delta V$

However, during the process of charging a capacitor, the voltage rises from 0 to max voltage linearly, depending on q:

$\Delta V = \frac{q}{c}$
$du = dq \frac{q}{c}$

We can then integrate all du wrt to dq to get total $\Delta U$ from neutral to charged, ie work stored in the capacitor:

$\Delta U = \displaystyle \int^Q_{0} \frac{q}{c} dq = \frac{1}{2} \frac{Q^2}{c}$

Where Q is defined as $v_{max}c$, typically set by the supplied voltage at the equipotential terminals.

Thus, we have 

$\Delta U = \frac{1}{2}c v^2$

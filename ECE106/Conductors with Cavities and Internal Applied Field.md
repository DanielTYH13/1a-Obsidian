*Key Concepts:*
___

Assume a conductor has an internal charge in it, offset at a distance r from the geometric center. 

We know that the net field inside the cavity must be 0 as the conductor is in equilibrium. In most cases, the only way for this to be true is if the interior is neutral. However, with a charge in the middle, a net zero field can be achieved with some amount of opposite charge on the wall, which the charge itself attracts from the conductor. 

The amount of this charge can be found using Gauss's law. Create a guassian surface surrounding the cavity. Since the E field is 0, then $Q_{internal} + Q_{walls}= 0$, ie there is an equal and opposite amount of charge on the cavity surface. 

This charge is drawn from the conductor walls themselves. Since the net E field of the cavity is 0 (And also the net charge is 0), the distribution of the outer walls are unaffected. Thus, we are left with this distribution:

The grounded case.

In the case that the conductor is grounded, all electrons taken from the conductor surface are replaced with those from the ground. 

In this case, the conductor shields the outside from the inside.

Way Two 

Imagine a mass moving through a field, for ever $dR$ in position it changes, the field can either exert a force opposing its movement or along its movement:

$dW = F \cdot \overline{dR}$

If the force is opposing the movement, then that means there is negative work done by the force. However, if the particle were ever to pass through the same path, but backwards, then the force would align with the direction, and thus enact positive work on the mass. Vice versa is true as well. 

In fact, since the force is conservative, the total work done returning from point b to a will always be the opposite of the work done from point a to b.

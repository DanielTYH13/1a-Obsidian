*Key Concepts:*
___

When a multivariate function of n variables is continuos at point $\vec{v}$, then it is sufficient to substitute to find the limit at that point. 

>[!definition]
>When a fcn is not continuous at said point, then it is not garunteed that all paths towards the point will approach the same limit. In these cases, the only way to prove a limit exists is using the squeeze theorem.
>

Recall that a limit only exists if all approaches to a given point converge to one value. Consider the one variable case: f(x).

In this case, given it is not continuous at x, then there exists two "ways" to approach x: from the left and from the right. In cases like these, it is feasable to determine the rh and lh limit.

Consider a scalar surface. There are infinite ways to approach x, in infinite shapes. It is not feasable to manually calculate all of said approaches.

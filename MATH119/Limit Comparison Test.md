*Key Concepts:*
___

Given two series, $\sum a_n$, $\sum b_n$, if the limit $\displaystyle \lim_{n \to \infty} \frac{a_n}{b_{n}}= L, 0 < L < \infty$, then $\sum a_{n}$ is convergent if and only if $\sum b_n$ is convergent.

It is an extension of the comparison test, which checks for multiplicity stricly greater than 1 or less than one. 

The LCT just checks if the multiplicity is finite, allowing you to extend an if and only if relationship in both directions. 

---
Common use cases:

- Similar behaving series which fail the comparison test, or are offset by some value. 
- Series that appear with a fundamental series on the numerator or denominator, which can be cancelled out to find an easy limit, and used to prove divergence / convergence.

---
Intuitiion:

You can imagine that the division of the two sequence terms is like a ratio between the two. 

If the ratio is a real number, then if one series is convergent, then the other series behaves like a scaled version of the first. 

If the ratio is 0 or infinity, then that means that regardless of if one of the series is convergent or divergent, the other one could be divergent or convergent, and still maintain the ratio (even if they both go to zero). 

The two values are just inverses of each other, and a product of which sequence is in the numerator

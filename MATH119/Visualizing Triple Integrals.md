*Key Concepts:*
___

Each time you integrate a nested integral, you are taking the sum of the function across that dimension specifically. 

To visualize this, imagine a sum across a single dimension, ending at the bounds defined by the other dimensions. Move this sum across the other dimensions to see how it changes in bounds. 

For every new integral you add, imagine taking the shape of the sum at a specific point in space, then extending it across the new dimension to a new bound, often also dependant on other dimensions. Move it around in space, and see how it changes depending on the bounds given.

Repeat. 

---
Example: Cartesian:

First its a line, which can be moved across a plane, and changes in bounds lengthwise. 

Then its a plane, which can be moved across a volume, which changes bounds lengthwise the same as the line, and widthwise with the new bounds.

Finally, imagine the 3d sum of the plane across a volume. 

This is what is happening when you are taking a triple integral. 

---
Example Spherical:

Depending on which you integrate first, in this example its phi then theta then radius.

First, imagine a sweeping cirlce along phi, with its bounds changing depending on its angle theta. 

Then, imagine a section of a sphere, with "bottom" bounds changing same as phi, and "side" bounds changing depending on r. 

Then imagine a volume of these spherical sections extended across in r space.

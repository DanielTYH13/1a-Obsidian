*Key Concepts:*
___

If two sequences, $A_{1, k}, A_{2, k}$ are identical for some sufficiently large k, then $A_{1, k}$ is convergent if and only if $A_{2, k}$ is convergent. 

---
Proof:

All partial sums are finite. The series 1 can be represented as the series 2 plus some partial sum. If series 1 is undefined, therefore so must series 2, as a partial sum is always defined. Similarily, if series 2 is undefined, so must series 1. 

Intuitive:

Imagine two series. The two series have some finite or non existent overlap.

Finite overlap:

One series can be represented as its own partial sum up until k_0, then the other series minus the overlap. 

If the other series is finite, then subtracting a finite partial sum and adding another will not make it infinite. Vice versa for infiniteness.

If the series itself is finite, then so must be the other series, minus the overlap. If it is infinite, then the series minus the overlap will also be infinite, thus making the other series infinite.

The same applies to no overlap.

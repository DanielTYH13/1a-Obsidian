Take a plane that lies on some point $\vec{r'} = (x', y', z')$, with normal vector $\vec{n} = (a, b, c)$. You must find an equation that relates the components of any point $\vec{r} = (x, y, z)$ together such that you can solve for z given x, y.

A neat way to do this is realizing that the vector $\vec{r} - \vec{r'}$ will always be orthogonal to $\vec{n}$. As such, $\vec{n}\cdot(\vec{r} - \vec{r'}) = 0$. From here you can derive $z = z' + A(x - x') + B(y - y')$

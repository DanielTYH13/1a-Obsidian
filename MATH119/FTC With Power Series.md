*Key Concepts:*
___

Given a function f(x) with f'(x) is easier to convert into a power series or taylor series, or is easier to work with in any kind, then you can utilize

$f(x) = \displaystyle \int \left( \frac{d}{dx} f(x) \right) dx$

In other words, you can convert the function into a function which is easier to turn to work with or approximate, then convert it back to its original form, giving an equivalent form or approximation. 

Then, you can find C at a given point by taking the limit of the function as the value approaches C (C for values of x infinitesimally close to $x_{0}$)

This is a great example of why thinking of integrals abstractly as antiderivatives is sometimes better than thinking of them as reimann sums. In this case, it makes no sense to think of the integral as an infinite sum of slices. Instead, it is better thought of as a tool that counteracts derivatives, with an abstract form.

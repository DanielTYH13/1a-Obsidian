*Key Concepts:*
___

A series is the sum of certain elements of a sequence. 

An infinite series is the sum of all of the elements of the sequence. 

A partial series is the sum of a finite set of the elements of the sequence. 

---
Convergence

A series is defined to be convergent if it approaches a finite value. We say that if

$\displaystyle \lim_{n \to \infty} \sum^n_{k = 0} {s_{n}} = s$, where s is finite and well defined, then it is convergent. 

If the sequence is defined for all of its elements, then all partial series will be finite. Thus, it does not make sense to ask whether a partial sum is convergent or divergent. 

Moreover, if a series is convergent, it does not matter where you start the series from. In other words,

if $\displaystyle \lim_{n \to \infty} \sum^n_{k = 0} s_{n} = s$, then $\displaystyle \lim_{n \to \infty} \sum^n_{k = j} s_{n} = s'$ for some j. You can imagine that if a sum is finite, then taking a few finite terms off will not make it infinite. Similarily, if a sum is infinite, then taking a few finite terms will not make it finite. 

---
Divergence

A series is divergent if it is not convergent. Ie

- Its limit is infinite
- Its limit is oscillating

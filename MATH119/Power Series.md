---

The power series has the form

$\displaystyle \sum^{\infty}_{n = 0} c_{n}(x-x_{0})^n$

If we define a function to be the power series to a given degree

$f(x) = c_{0} + c_{1}(x - x_{0}) + c_{2}(x - x_{0})^2 + c_{3}(x - x_{0})^3 + \cdots c_{n}(x-x_{0})^n$

(In fact, all polynomials can be represented in this form.)

Then, for any given value of x, the function is finite or 0 (converges) or is infinite (diverges), according to the power series's divergence at that x value. Thus, we can gain information about function behavior from series properties.

---
Why is the power series important? Why should we know about it?

The power series is the general form of the taylor series. However, instead of the taylor coefficients, we just define them as any coefficient. 

Thus, knowing the properties of the power series can help us approximate or find exact solutions to the properties of many other otherwise difficult to manipulate functions by first approximating them with a taylor series.

---
What is the difference between the power series and the taylor series?

The power series follows no rules. The set of taylor series is a subset of the set of power series.

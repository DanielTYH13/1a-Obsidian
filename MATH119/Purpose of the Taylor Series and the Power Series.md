Approximation (From an engineering standpoint):

It is much easier to work out a polynomial than to use complex functions like roots, logarithms, etc. Thus, for low computational strength scenarios, accuracy can be traded for speed and simplicity.

---
Form conversion

Most functions can be expressed as an infinite power series (Similar to how we proved error tends to 0 for taylor series of some functions). These functions can be represented **completely analytically as a polynomial of infinite degree.** 

Many operations such as integration, derivation, limits, etc, are very difficult with roots, logarithms, factorials, etc. 

Instead, we can convert to polynomials and work from there, making things easier. 

For certain scenarios, where the operation is at a specific point (The centering of the taylor series, for example) where error goes to zero, then a finite sum of polynomials can be used as an exact representation of the function **around** that point. 

Thus, we keep equivalence and reduced complexity.

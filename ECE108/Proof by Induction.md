*Key Concepts:*
___

Proof by induction is when you establish that $p' \implies q' \iff p \implies q$.

You prove a base case, and the step, and the step proves the base case for the next step, and thus the next, and so on so forth. 

This continues for the entire domain of discourse.

---
Weak induction:

Weak induction is when you show that the step is true for only one step. I.e, it does not require the premise to be true for all previous steps. 

It is used in cases where the step relies only on the previous case.

---
Strong Induction:

Strong induction is when you specify the step is valid if and only the premise is true for all previous steps. 

It is used when you need to bring previous steps into the proof.

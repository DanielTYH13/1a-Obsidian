$\binom{n}{k} = \binom{n}{n-k}$

---

Imagine you want to choose 3 from a set of 10. This is equivalent to choosing 7 NOT to be in the set of 3 from the set of 10. Ie, choosing a set vs choosing its complement is the same. 

Also, by the definition of choose:

$\binom{n}{k} = \frac{n!}{(n-k)!k!}$, you can see that the bottom terms "switch places" for $\binom{n}{n-k}$ and are equal.

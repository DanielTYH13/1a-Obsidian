*Key Concepts:*
___

Relations are sets of ordered pairs. If $\langle a, b \rangle \in R$, then a and b are said to be related under R.

Order matters, hence the ordered pairs.

Relations are defined by a rule and a set. It is important to note that the relation is just the set of all ordered pairs, as a subset of $S^2$, and are defined by the rule ([[Set Builder Condition|Set Builder Notation]]). Thus, relations are specific to a superset. 

This is important to specify as the same rule applied to different sets can cause relations with different characteristics, such as one relation being reflexive or transitive, or symmetric, and the other not.

---

It is important to note that relations do not behave like functions, in which an argument is taken and an output is given. While this is possible, it is unpractical and a bad way to think of it. Instead, it is better to think of a relation more as a graph of connections. 

For example, take the less than relation. Ask yourself, does it make sense to ask "what is the output of $2<$"? An output of the interval $(2, \infty)$ may be a valid answer, but is cumbersome and doesn't really tell us much.

But ask, is 2 related to 3 ($\langle 2, 3 \rangle$) under $R_{<}$? The answer is yes, as $\langle 2, 3\rangle$ is in the set $R_{<}$. And the meaning of the relation set, $R_{<}$, in this case, is that 2 is less than 3. 

---
Classifications of Relations:

- [[Symmetric Relations]]
- [[Asymmetric Relations]]
- [[Antisymmetric Relations]]
- [[Reflexive Relations]]

Posets are reflexive, antisymmetric, and transitive

Strict Partial Orders are irreflexive, asymmetric, and transitive. 

Equivalences are reflexive, symmetric, and transitive. 

---
Properties of relations:

Functions are a subset of relations. Ie, relations can sometimes be functions, and sometimes not. 

Relations are a subset of the cartesian cross product between their domain and codomain.

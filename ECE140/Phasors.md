*Key Concepts:*
___

In cases where all signals operate at the same frequency, the phase shift between two signals will always remain constant. So too its magnitude. 

Phasors are a way of representing the phase difference / shift, and amplitude of a signal in complex exponential form, which borrows many of the helpful sinusoidal properties from this number system. 

---
For example, take an impedent circuit, where current lags behind voltage some phase shift theta. 

By creating a phasor for both signals, we encapsulate how large the two signals are, and how far apart they are from a point of reference. 

Now, say we would like to find the magnitude of and phase shift of the power. It is a property in cartesian signals that the multiplication of two signals will create a new signal with resultant magnitude the product of two, and phase shift the sum of the two. 

But in cartesian signals, this is a cumbersome process. It turns out that the same effect is true for complex exponential functions! And since we don't care about the time factor, we can just factor it out and delete it. 

So with phasors, we can perform the exact same operation, and obtain a new phasor telling us the new amplitude and phase shift of the resultant signal.

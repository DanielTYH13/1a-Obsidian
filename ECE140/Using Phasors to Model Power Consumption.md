*Key Concepts:*
___

We can also derive power using phasors. **Note that we cannot say anything about instantaneous power, as phasors only describe phase shift.**

$V = V_{rms}\angle \theta_v$
$I = I_{rms} \angle \theta_{i}$

$P = V_{rms}I_{rms}\angle(\theta_{v}+ \theta_{i}) = V_{rms}I_{rms}^* = P  + jQ$

Ie, this creates a triangle with hypotenuse of $V_{rms} I_{rms}$, with real and imaginary side lengths. 

---
Why are the side lengths equivalent to real power and reactive power?

To find the instantaneous power delivered, we must reintroduce the time factor, by [[Phasors|the definition of a phasor]]. And since the phasor is in the imaginary plane, it emerges from the math that there must be a real and reactive part of power!

Comparing to the trigonemetric general solution for power from earlier, we can prove (proof omitted here) that these side lengths correlate to the P and Q factors.

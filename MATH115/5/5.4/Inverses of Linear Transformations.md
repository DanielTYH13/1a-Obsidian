*Key Concepts:*
___

- Given that T, S are transformations s.t. 
	- $T \circ S = Id = S \circ T$, then 
	- T is invertible and S is a inverse of T

- Note that the inverse of a linear transformation is unique: Given S, R are inverses of T, then:
	- $S = S \circ I_{d}= S \circ (T \circ R) = (S \circ T) \circ R = I_{d} \circ R = R$
	- $S = R$
	- Therefore the inverse must be unique. 

- Theorem: Given S, T be linear transformations with the same domain and codomain, then $S = T^{-1}$ iaoi $[S] = [T]^{-1}$.
	- In particular, T is invertible iaoi $[T]$ is invertible.
		- This follows directly from the definition of the inverse of a transformation. 
		- Ie, $[T^{-1}] = [T]^{-1}$
- This stems from the definition of the standard matrix for [[Composition of Linear Transformations]] and the [[The Matrix Inverse]].
